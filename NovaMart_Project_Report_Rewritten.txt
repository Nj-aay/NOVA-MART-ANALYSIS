NOVAMART RETAIL
SALES AND PROFITABILITY INVESTIGATION

PROJECT TITLE
Why Are Sales Growing but Profit Falling? — A Sales and Profitability Investigation for NovaMart Retail

ROLE
Sales and Business Data Analyst

BUSINESS
NovaMart Retail is a fictional Nigerian consumer retail business.

REPORTING PERIOD
January–December 2025

DATASET
7,500 sales transactions


1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

NovaMart Retail management raised a simple but important concern: the business was receiving more orders, but the increase in sales activity was not translating into the expected level of profit.

This investigation examines revenue, profit, margins, products, customers, sales channels and discounts to determine what changed during 2025 and where management should focus its attention.

The H1 versus H2 results show that NovaMart's main problem is profitability. Revenue declined from approximately NGN351.45 million in H1 2025 to NGN325.79 million in H2, a 7.3% decline. Profit fell from approximately NGN82.29 million to NGN55.16 million, a 32.9% decline.

The difference between these two declines is significant. Profit fell much faster than revenue, showing that NovaMart was retaining substantially less profit from its sales in H2.

The channel results reinforce this. Online orders increased by 2.6%, while online profit fell by 32.5%. Social Media also weakened, with orders down 9.7%, revenue down 14.7% and profit down 36.4%.

At product level, Premium Smartphones sold 1.0% more units but generated 44.4% less profit. Wireless Earbuds showed a similar volume-to-profit gap: quantity increased from 767 to 848 and revenue increased from approximately NGN21.80 million to NGN22.25 million, but profit fell from approximately NGN5.67 million to NGN4.46 million. The reported discount also increased from 36.95 to 65.65.

The results show that NovaMart should not measure growth using orders, units or revenue alone. Pricing, discounts, product mix and channel profitability need to be reviewed together.

5. PROJECT METHODOLOGY

The project followed the complete analysis workflow:

RAW DATA → CLEANING → ANALYSIS → DASHBOARD → INSIGHTS → RECOMMENDATIONS

DATA CLEANING AND VALIDATION

The transaction data was prepared for analysis by reviewing the dataset structure, checking data quality and validating the fields required for the calculations and comparisons.

FINANCIAL ANALYSIS

Revenue, cost, profit and profit margin were examined to understand whether sales activity was translating into financial performance.

SALES ANALYSIS

Orders and units sold were compared across periods to determine whether changes in volume explained the movement in revenue and profit.

TIME ANALYSIS

Performance was compared between H1 and H2 2025. Monthly performance was also included in the investigation to identify changes throughout the year.

PRODUCT ANALYSIS

Products and categories were compared using sales and profitability measures. This prevents high-volume products from being treated as successful when their profit contribution is weak.

CHANNEL ANALYSIS

Online, Physical Store and Social Media were compared using orders, revenue and profit.

CUSTOMER ANALYSIS

New and returning customers were considered to determine whether customer mix and purchasing behaviour could be associated with the profitability decline.

DISCOUNT ANALYSIS

Discounts were examined because changes in discounting can increase sales activity while reducing profit per transaction.

DASHBOARD DEVELOPMENT

The results were brought together in a management dashboard comparing H1 and H2 performance and highlighting the main issues and actions.

INTERPRETATION

Revenue decreased by 7.3%, while profit decreased by 32.9%. The decline in profit was therefore much larger than the decline in revenue.

This indicates that the business was not only selling less in H2; it was also generating less profit from the sales it made.

For management, the implication is straightforward: increasing order volume without protecting margins may not solve the underlying problem.


8. PROFIT MARGIN

The dashboard reports an H2 profit margin of 18.7%.

Profit margin measures the amount of profit retained from revenue. It is important because a business can generate substantial revenue while its profitability weakens.

Factors that can place pressure on margin include:

- Higher discounts
- Lower selling prices
- Changes in product mix
- Higher costs
- Lower-margin sales channels
- Increased sales of products with weaker margins

NovaMart should therefore monitor profit margin alongside revenue, orders and units sold.

11. DISCOUNT AND PROFITABILITY ANALYSIS

Discounting is a key area for management review.

A discount can increase sales volume, but if the reduction in selling price is greater than the additional profit created by the extra volume, the promotion can reduce overall profitability.

The Wireless Earbuds results illustrate this relationship. The reported discount increased from 36.95 in H1 to 65.65 in H2. Quantity increased from 767 to 848 and revenue increased from approximately NGN21.80 million to NGN22.25 million. Despite this, profit decreased from approximately NGN5.67 million to NGN4.46 million.

NovaMart should therefore assess promotions using profit as a core measure, not only sales volume.

Questions for ongoing discount analysis should include:

- Which products receive the largest discounts?
- Which discounts generate additional volume?
- Which promotions reduce profit?
- Which products remain profitable after discounting?
- How does discount level relate to profit margin?

16. RECOMMENDATIONS

RECOMMENDATION 1: REVIEW PRICING AND DISCOUNT STRATEGIES

NovaMart should identify products where discounts reduce profit without generating enough additional sales to compensate.

Management should:

- Set discount limits.
- Review heavily discounted products.
- Compare profit before and after promotions.
- Test lower discount levels where appropriate.
- Redesign promotions that consistently weaken profitability.

RECOMMENDATION 2: PRIORITIZE PROFITABLE PRODUCTS

Products should be evaluated using both sales performance and profitability.

NovaMart should identify:

- High-volume, high-profit products
- High-volume, low-profit products
- Low-volume, high-profit products
- Low-volume, low-profit products

The Premium Smartphone result demonstrates why this classification is necessary.

RECOMMENDATION 3: INVESTIGATE THE ONLINE PROFIT DECLINE

The 2.6% increase in online orders alongside a 32.5% decline in online profit requires detailed investigation.

Management should examine discounts, product mix, average selling price, profit per order, customer type, product margins and online transaction costs.

RECOMMENDATION 4: REASSESS SOCIAL MEDIA PERFORMANCE

Social Media should be reviewed using both sales and financial measures. Management should examine campaign performance, promotional costs, discount levels, products promoted, orders, revenue and profit.

RECOMMENDATION 5: TRACK PROFIT MARGIN ALONGSIDE SALES

Regular management reporting should include:

- Revenue
- Profit
- Profit margin
- Orders
- Units sold
- Profit per order
- Discount level

RECOMMENDATION 6: SEGMENT NEW AND RETURNING CUSTOMERS

Customer groups should be compared by profitability, not simply customer count. This will show which groups generate stronger margins and how their purchasing behaviour differs.

RECOMMENDATION 7: SET PROFITABILITY TARGETS FOR PROMOTIONS

Each major promotion should have defined targets for product, customer, expected orders, expected revenue, expected profit, maximum discount and minimum acceptable margin. Actual results should be compared with those targets after the promotion.

19. BUSINESS IMPACT

The analysis shows why NovaMart should change the way it evaluates performance.

A conventional sales report may focus on orders, units sold and revenue. These measures are useful, but they do not show whether the business is making enough money from those sales.

NovaMart's results provide several examples:

- Online orders increased, but online profit fell.
- Premium Smartphone units increased, but product profit fell.
- Wireless Earbuds quantity and revenue increased, but profit fell.

The business therefore needs a profitability-focused reporting approach.

The important question is not only:

“How much did we sell?”

It is also:

“How much profit did we generate from what we sold?”


20. CONCLUSION

The NovaMart Retail investigation shows a clear deterioration in profitability during H2 2025.

Revenue declined by 7.3%, from approximately NGN351.45 million in H1 to NGN325.79 million in H2.

Profit declined by 32.9%, from approximately NGN82.29 million to NGN55.16 million.

The much larger decline in profit shows that NovaMart's problem extends beyond the amount of revenue generated.

Online orders increased by 2.6%, but online profit declined by 32.5%. Social Media also recorded significant declines, with orders falling by 9.7%, revenue falling by 14.7% and profit falling by 36.4%.

Premium Smartphones sold 1.0% more units but generated 44.4% less profit.

Wireless Earbuds generated more quantity and slightly more revenue in H2, but profit declined from approximately NGN5.67 million to NGN4.46 million while the reported discount increased from 36.95 to 65.65.

These results show that NovaMart should not rely on sales volume as its main measure of growth. Pricing, discounts, product mix and channel economics need to be reviewed together.

The immediate priority is to identify where margin is being lost, understand why online sales are becoming less profitable, review Social Media performance, and tighten the way discounts and product profitability are managed.

The central business finding is:

NOVAMART'S MAIN CHALLENGE IS NOT SIMPLY GENERATING SALES. IT IS TURNING SALES INTO PROFITABLE GROWTH.
