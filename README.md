1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

NovaMart Retail is a fictional Nigerian consumer retail business that sells consumer products through multiple sales channels. Management raised a business concern: “We are getting more orders, but we are not making as much money as expected. We need to understand what is happening and what we should do next.”

The investigation was designed to move from raw transaction data through cleaning, analysis, dashboard development, insight generation and practical recommendations.

The dataset contains 7,500 sales transactions covering January–December 2025.

The H1 versus H2 analysis shows that NovaMart's main issue is not simply sales volume. Revenue declined while profit declined much more sharply. Revenue fell from approximately NGN351.45 million in H1 to NGN325.79 million in H2, a decline of 7.3%. Profit fell from approximately NGN82.29 million to NGN55.16 million, a much larger decline of 32.9%.

H2 profit margin was 18.7%, showing that a smaller share of revenue was being retained as profit.

The channel analysis also reveals a major profitability concern. Online orders increased by 2.6%, but online profit fell by 32.5%. This indicates that higher online order activity did not translate into equivalent profitability.

Social Media was the weakest-performing channel in the dashboard comparison. Orders fell by 9.7%, revenue fell by 14.7%, and profit fell by 36.4%.

Product-level analysis provides another warning sign. Premium Smartphones sold slightly more units (+1.0%) but generated 44.4% less profit. This demonstrates that sales volume alone was not enough to protect profitability.

The evidence therefore points toward margin pressure, discounting/pricing issues, and differences in channel and product profitability rather than a simple lack-of-sales problem.

The recommended management response is to review discounting and pricing strategies, prioritize profitable products and channels rather than sales volume alone, investigate the reason online profit is falling despite higher order activity, and reassess Social Media sales performance and promotional effectiveness.

5. ANALYTICAL APPROACH
The investigation followed a business-analysis workflow.

STEP 1 — DATA CLEANING AND VALIDATION

The first stage of the project is to ensure that the transaction data is suitable for analysis. This includes checking the structure and quality of the data, validating fields, checking for inconsistencies and preparing the dataset for analysis.

The purpose of cleaning is to reduce the risk of producing misleading business conclusions from incomplete, duplicated, inconsistent or incorrectly formatted records.

STEP 2 — KPI ANALYSIS

Core business KPIs were calculated and compared across periods.

These include:

- Revenue
- Profit
- Profit Margin
- Orders
- Units Sold

STEP 3 — TIME-BASED ANALYSIS

Performance was investigated across the year and compared between:

- H1 2025
- H2 2025

This makes it possible to identify whether the business deteriorated gradually or whether the main change occurred in the second half of the year.

STEP 4 — PRODUCT ANALYSIS

Products were compared using sales and profitability measures.

This prevents management from judging products based only on units sold or revenue.

STEP 5 — CHANNEL ANALYSIS

The performance of Online, Physical Store and Social Media channels was compared using:

- Orders
- Revenue
- Profit

STEP 6 — CUSTOMER ANALYSIS

New versus returning customer activity was considered to understand customer behavior and whether changes in customer mix could be associated with the profitability problem.

STEP 7 — DISCOUNT ANALYSIS

Discounting was investigated because aggressive discounts can increase sales activity while reducing the amount of profit generated per sale.

STEP 8 — DASHBOARD DEVELOPMENT

The findings were consolidated into a management dashboard comparing H1 and H2 performance and highlighting the most important business issues.

8. PROFIT MARGIN


H2 profit margin shown on the dashboard:
18.7%

Profit margin is important because it measures the amount of profit retained from revenue.

A business can increase sales while becoming less profitable if:

- Prices are reduced too aggressively.
- Discounts are too high.
- Product mix shifts toward lower-margin products.
- Costs increase.
- Certain channels become less profitable.
- High-volume products generate relatively little profit.

NovaMart's H2 performance demonstrates why revenue and profit should always be reviewed together.

The dashboard specifically recommends tracking profit margin alongside sales performance.

11. DISCOUNTING AND PROFITABILITY

Discounting is one of the key areas identified in the project brief and dashboard recommendations.

The investigation indicates that discounting should be reviewed because discounts can create a situation where sales volume increases while profit per transaction decreases.

The Wireless Earbuds example illustrates this pattern:

Discount:
H1 — 36.95
H2 — 65.65

At the same time:

Quantity increased from 767 to 848.

Revenue increased from approximately NGN21.80 million to NGN22.25 million.

Profit decreased from approximately NGN5.67 million to NGN4.46 million.

This is a critical business lesson:

More units + more revenue does not necessarily mean more profit.

Management should therefore evaluate discounts against incremental profit rather than evaluating promotions only by the number of sales they generate.

16. RECOMMENDATIONS

RECOMMENDATION 1 — REVIEW PRICING AND DISCOUNT STRATEGIES

NovaMart should review its current discount structure and identify products where discounts are reducing profit without generating enough additional volume to compensate.

Discounts should be evaluated based on incremental profit, not just incremental orders.

Management should consider:

- Setting discount limits.
- Reviewing heavily discounted products.
- Measuring profit before and after promotions.
- Testing smaller discounts.
- Removing promotions that consistently reduce profit.

RECOMMENDATION 2 — PRIORITIZE PROFITABLE PRODUCTS

NovaMart should identify products that generate strong profit and products that generate high sales but weak profit.

The Premium Smartphone example demonstrates why this is important.

Management should track:

- Units sold
- Revenue
- Profit
- Profit margin
- Discount
- Average selling price

Products should not be judged on sales volume alone.

RECOMMENDATION 3 — INVESTIGATE ONLINE PROFIT DECLINE

Online orders increased by 2.6%, yet online profit declined by 32.5%.

Management should investigate this channel in detail.

The analysis should examine:

- Average discount
- Product mix
- Average order value
- Profit per order
- Product-level margins
- Customer type
- Cost associated with online transactions

The goal should be to identify why additional orders are not producing additional profit.

RECOMMENDATION 4 — REASSESS SOCIAL MEDIA PERFORMANCE

Social Media recorded declines across orders, revenue and profit, with profit declining by 36.4%.

NovaMart should review:

- Social Media campaign performance
- Promotional spending
- Discount levels
- Products promoted
- Conversion performance
- Profit generated by campaigns

Promotions that generate activity without acceptable profit should be redesigned or discontinued.

RECOMMENDATION 5 — TRACK PROFIT MARGIN ALONGSIDE SALES

Management dashboards should display revenue and profit together.

A sales increase should not automatically be considered positive if profit margin is falling.

Core management KPIs should include:

- Revenue
- Profit
- Profit margin
- Orders
- Units sold
- Profit per order
- Discount rate

RECOMMENDATION 6 — SEGMENT CUSTOMERS

NovaMart should compare new and returning customers based on profitability, not just customer count.

This will help identify which customer groups generate stronger margins and which groups are more discount-dependent.

RECOMMENDATION 7 — CREATE A PROFITABILITY-FOCUSED PROMOTION FRAMEWORK

Before launching a promotion, NovaMart should define:

- Target product
- Target customer
- Expected orders
- Expected revenue
- Expected profit
- Maximum acceptable discount
- Minimum acceptable margin

After the promotion, actual results should be compared with these targets.

19. BUSINESS IMPACT

The investigation changes the way NovaMart's performance should be evaluated.

A basic sales report might focus on:

- Number of orders
- Number of units
- Revenue

However, this investigation shows that these metrics can hide profitability problems.

NovaMart needs to move toward a profitability-focused performance framework.

For example:

More orders are not automatically better.

More units are not automatically better.

A successful commercial outcome should be evaluated using the relationship between sales activity and the profit generated from that activity.

20. CONCLUSION

NovaMart's H1 versus H2 2025 performance reveals a clear profitability challenge.

Revenue declined by 7.3%, from approximately NGN351.45 million to NGN325.79 million.

Profit declined by 32.9%, from approximately NGN82.29 million to NGN55.16 million.

The much larger decline in profit indicates that the business problem extends beyond sales volume.

Online orders increased by 2.6%, but online profit fell by 32.5%.

Social Media performance also deteriorated, with orders falling by 9.7%, revenue falling by 14.7% and profit falling by 36.4%.

At the product level, Premium Smartphones sold 1.0% more units but generated 44.4% less profit.

The Wireless Earbuds example further demonstrates the relationship between discounting, volume and profitability: quantity and revenue increased in H2 while profit decreased, alongside a substantial increase in the reported discount figure.

The overall story is therefore:

NOVA MART DOES NOT SIMPLY HAVE A SALES PROBLEM.
IT HAS A PROFITABILITY PROBLEM.

Management should respond by reviewing discounts and pricing, investigating channel economics, prioritizing profitable products, reassessing Social Media performance and monitoring profit margin alongside sales volume.

The central lesson from the analysis is that growth in orders or revenue should not be treated as successful growth unless the business is also generating sustainable profit.
